> **Quick answer:** For residential property in Ontario, you first file a **Request for Reconsideration (RfR)** with MPAC, using the deadline printed on your Property Assessment Notice. If you disagree with MPAC's decision, you generally have **90 days** from the date MPAC notifies you to appeal to the **Assessment Review Board (ARB)**. The winning evidence is sales of comparable homes around MPAC's valuation date — January 1, 2016 for the 2026 tax year — not today's prices.

## First: is your assessment actually too high?

Your assessment will almost certainly be lower than today's market value, because 2026 assessments are still based on **January 1, 2016** values. That alone isn't grounds for an appeal. The real question is:

> Would my home, in its current condition, have sold for less than its assessed value on January 1, 2016 — compared with similar homes?

Signs it might be worth checking:

- Similar homes on your street are assessed noticeably lower
- MPAC's records are wrong (square footage, lot size, number of bathrooms, finished basement, garage)
- Your home has a problem MPAC doesn't know about (structural issues, busy road, hydro corridor, flooding)
- A recent improvement was valued too generously

## Step 1: Check your property profile

Log in to MPAC's AboutMyProperty portal and review the details MPAC holds. Many successful reconsiderations are simply factual corrections — a basement recorded as finished when it isn't, or the wrong living area.

## Step 2: File a Request for Reconsideration

- Use the deadline on your notice (often March 31 of the tax year, or 120 days from the notice date for some notices — always check yours)
- Explain what's wrong and attach evidence
- MPAC reviews and sends a written decision

## Step 3: Appeal to the Assessment Review Board if needed

If you disagree with MPAC's RfR decision, file an appeal with the ARB (part of Tribunals Ontario). The deadline is normally **90 days** from the date of MPAC's decision letter, and the letter confirms it. Hearings are more formal; evidence and preparation matter.

## Evidence that works

| Strong evidence | Weak evidence |
|---|---|
| Sales of similar homes close to Jan 1, 2016 | Today's listing prices |
| Assessments of truly comparable neighbours | "My taxes went up" |
| Proof of incorrect property data | Comparisons with very different homes |
| Photos and reports showing defects | General complaints about the economy |
| A retrospective appraisal as of the valuation date | An agent's opinion of today's price |

## Where an appraisal helps

A **retrospective appraisal as of January 1, 2016** gives the ARB an independent, standards-based opinion of what your property was worth on the legislated date. It is most useful when the dispute is about value rather than a simple data error, or when the potential tax savings justify the cost. ## Frequently asked questions

### Will appealing raise my assessment?
MPAC can confirm, lower or, in some cases, increase an assessment on review. Make sure your evidence genuinely supports a lower value before you file.

### Do I need a lawyer or tax agent?
Not for an RfR. Many homeowners handle it themselves. For an ARB hearing with significant money at stake, professional help can be worthwhile.

### Can I still appeal if I missed the deadline?
Late filings are limited. Contact MPAC or the ARB promptly and ask what options exist for your situation.

### Does a successful appeal refund past taxes?
It typically affects the tax years covered by the appeal. Ask MPAC or the ARB which years your filing applies to.
