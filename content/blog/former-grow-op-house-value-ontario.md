> **Quick answer:** A home that was used as an illegal grow operation can sell for less even after repairs, because of potential **mould, structural or electrical damage**, **financing and insurance hurdles**, and **buyer stigma**. Proper remediation with documentation reduces the discount. Appraisers consider both the physical condition and how the market reacts, using sales of similar properties where available.

## Why the history matters

Illegal grow operations often involved:

- High humidity, leading to **mould** in walls, attics and framing
- **Electrical bypasses** and overloaded wiring
- Structural changes — vents cut through roofs, walls or foundations
- Chemical residue

Even after cleanup, some buyers and lenders remain cautious.

## Remediation documentation that helps

- Municipal records showing the property was cleared and any orders closed
- Environmental or mould remediation reports with post-remediation testing
- Electrical Safety Authority inspection after repairs
- Building permits for structural repairs
- Invoices from qualified contractors

The stronger the paper trail, the smaller the stigma tends to be.

## Financing and insurance

Some lenders and insurers ask about or restrict former grow-ops. Buyers may face extra questions, higher costs or fewer options. That narrower buyer pool is part of why values can be lower.

## How appraisers approach it

1. **Physical condition** — is remediation complete and documented?
2. **Market reaction** — sales of other known former grow-ops, if any, and how much they differed from comparable homes
3. **Time** — stigma can fade over time and with ownership changes
4. **Disclosure** — the report notes the history and the assumptions made

## Disclosure

Sellers and their agents should get legal advice on disclosure. Hiding known material issues can lead to lawsuits after closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a former grow-op ever sell at full value?
With thorough remediation, documentation and time, the discount can become small. Results vary by area and buyer pool.

### How do I find out if a house was a grow-op?
Ask direct questions, check with the municipality and police where records are available, and look for signs during an inspection. A lawyer can advise on searches.

### Will a lender order a special inspection?
Some may require environmental testing or additional reports.
