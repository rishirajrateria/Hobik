> **Quick answer:** In the GTA, upgrades that add **usable space, function or income** tend to hold value best: finished basements with good ceiling height, an extra bathroom, legal secondary suites, kitchens in dated homes, and parking where it's scarce. Highly personal or luxury finishes often return less than they cost. Appraisers value what the market pays, not what you spent.

## Cost isn't value

A $150,000 kitchen doesn't add $150,000. Appraisers ask: *What would a typical buyer in this neighbourhood pay for this home with the upgrade versus without it?* The answer comes from comparable sales, not invoices.

## Upgrades that tend to hold value

| Upgrade | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Extra bathroom** | Fixes a common functional shortfall in older homes |
| **Finished basement** (good height, light) | Adds usable space buyers compare directly |
| **Legal secondary suite** | Adds income and lender-recognised rent |
| **Kitchen refresh in a dated home** | Brings the home up to market expectations |
| **Parking** (where legal and scarce) | Major value factor in older Toronto neighbourhoods |
| **Roof, windows, HVAC** | Don't add glamour, but avoid deductions for deferred maintenance |

## Upgrades that often under-perform

- Very high-end finishes in a mid-range neighbourhood (over-improvement)
- Pools in areas where buyers see them as maintenance
- Highly personal design choices
- Converting bedrooms into other uses (reducing bedroom count)

## Neighbourhood ceilings

Every area has a practical ceiling. Once a home is the most improved on its street, each additional dollar tends to return less.

## How to make sure renovations are counted

- Keep invoices and dates
- Pull permits — unpermitted work is treated cautiously
- Give the appraiser a list of upgrades
- Finish projects before the inspection if you can

## Frequently asked questions

### Do energy upgrades add value?
They can, especially where buyers value lower bills, but the market evidence varies. See [heat pumps and solar panels](/blog/heat-pump-solar-panels-home-value-ontario).

### Should I renovate before selling?
Small repairs and refreshes often make sense. Big renovations right before selling rarely pay for themselves.

### Does a renovation change my effective age?
Yes. Updating major systems and finishes makes a home "function younger," which appraisers reflect.
