> **Quick answer:** Condo appraisals lean heavily on **recent sales in the same building**, then adjust for differences in **size, floor, view and exposure, layout, parking, locker, finishes and maintenance fees**. The building itself — its age, amenities, reserve fund and reputation — affects every unit in it.

## Same-building sales come first

Nothing is more comparable than a similar unit in the same tower. When enough exist, appraisers rely on them heavily. When they don't (new or small buildings), they look at nearby buildings of similar age, quality and location.

## Unit-level factors

| Factor | What appraisers consider |
|---|---|
| **Size and layout** | Square footage, split vs. non-split bedrooms, wasted corridor space, dens that can or can't be bedrooms |
| **Floor** | Higher floors usually sell for more, but not always — penthouse collections differ |
| **View and exposure** | Unobstructed lake or skyline views versus facing another tower; south light |
| **Parking** | One of the biggest adjustments downtown — can be worth tens of thousands |
| **Locker** | Smaller, but still valued, especially in larger units |
| **Outdoor space** | Terraces and large balconies carry a premium |
| **Finishes** | Upgraded kitchens and floors versus original builder finishes |
| **Condition** | Wear, renovations and maintenance |

## Building-level factors

- Age and construction quality
- Amenities (gym, concierge, pool, party room)
- Maintenance fees relative to similar buildings
- Reserve fund health and any special assessments
- Rental restrictions or short-term rental rules
- Ongoing litigation or major repairs

These are often found in the **status certificate**, which is worth having on hand.

## Assignment sales and new condos

Pre-construction assignments add complexity: the unit may not have registered, comparable resales may not exist yet, and the closing date is in the future. Appraisers rely on comparable new buildings and resale data nearby. See our guide to [appraising assignment sales](/blog/assignment-sale-appraisal-ontario).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do maintenance fees lower my condo's value?
High fees relative to similar buildings can reduce what buyers will pay. Appraisers look at fees in context — what they cover and how they compare.

### Is a condo appraisal faster than a house appraisal?
Often, because same-building sales make the research more direct. Unique units (penthouses, large terraces) can take longer.

### Does a parking spot really matter that much?
In downtown Toronto, yes. Many buyers won't consider a unit without parking, and those who will often expect a lower price.
