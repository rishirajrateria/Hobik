> **Quick answer:** Ravine lots often sell at a **premium** for privacy, greenery and views. But parts of the lot may be regulated by the **City of Toronto's ravine protection rules** and the **Toronto and Region Conservation Authority (TRCA)**, which can limit additions, pools, decks, tree removal and rebuild footprints. Appraisers weigh the premium against the usable lot area and building limits.

## Why ravine lots are different

- **Private, green outlook** — no rear neighbours
- **Natural features** — mature trees, wildlife, trails
- **Regulated areas** — slopes, valleys, flood hazards and setbacks
- **Usable area** — a big lot on paper may have a small flat yard

## What can be restricted

Depending on where the regulated limit falls:

- Rear additions and new houses close to the top of bank
- Pools, decks, retaining walls and sheds
- Grading and fill
- Removing or injuring protected trees

Some lots need permits from both the City and TRCA before work.

## How appraisers value ravine lots

1. **Comparable ravine sales** in the same area — the clearest evidence of the premium
2. **Usable vs. total lot area** — steep or regulated portions count differently
3. **Rebuild potential** — especially where buyers purchase to rebuild
4. **Hazards** — erosion, slope stability, flood risk

## Questions to ask before buying

- Where is the regulated limit on this lot?
- Have previous owners had permits approved or refused?
- Is there any erosion or slope-stability history?
- Are trees protected?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is every ravine lot worth more?
Usually there's a premium, but regulation, slopes and usable space can reduce or even reverse it for some buyers.

### Can I build a pool on a ravine lot?
Possibly, but it may require approvals and may not be permitted near the slope. Check with the City and TRCA.

### Do appraisers need a survey?
A survey showing the lot, structures and regulated areas is very helpful.
