> **Quick answer:** Yes. Start by checking the report for **factual errors** (size, bedrooms, basement, upgrades). Then gather **better comparable sales** the appraiser may have missed. For a lender's appraisal, submit a **reconsideration request** through your broker or lender with specific evidence. Disagreeing with the number isn't enough — you need facts.

## Error vs. disagreement

| Type | Example | Likely outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Factual error** | Wrong square footage, missing bathroom | Usually corrected quickly |
| **Missed information** | Recent renovation not disclosed | May change value with proof |
| **Better comparables** | Closer, more similar recent sales | May be considered |
| **Disagreement** | "It's worth more" | Rarely changes anything |

## Building a strong request

1. List specific errors with proof (survey, floor plan, photos, permits)
2. Provide up to three to five comparable sales — recent, nearby, similar
3. Explain why each is a better match
4. Include renovation invoices and dates
5. Keep it factual and concise

## If it's a lender appraisal

You usually can't contact the appraiser directly. Your broker or lender submits the request. If it fails, you may be able to apply with a different lender, which may order a new appraisal.

## If it's an appraisal you ordered

Contact the appraiser directly. They'll review new information and explain their reasoning. If you still disagree, you can get a second opinion — but expect a different appraiser to reach a similar range if the evidence is solid.

## Complaints

If you believe an appraiser acted unprofessionally, the Appraisal Institute of Canada has a complaints process for its members. That's for conduct issues, not simple value disagreements.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a reconsideration take?
Often a few days, depending on the lender and appraiser.

### Will a second appraisal always be higher?
No. If the first was well supported, a second may be similar.

### Should I tell the appraiser what value I need?
No — appraisers must be independent and can't work to a target value.
