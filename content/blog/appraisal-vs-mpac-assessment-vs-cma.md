> **Quick answer:** An **appraisal** is an independent, standards-based opinion of one property's market value on a specific date. An **MPAC assessment** is a mass valuation used to calculate property tax — and for the 2026 tax year it is still based on January 1, 2016 values. A **CMA** is a free pricing estimate from a real estate agent who hopes to list your home. Lenders, courts, executors and the CRA rely on appraisals.

## Side-by-side

| | Certified appraisal | MPAC assessment | Realtor CMA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prepared by** | Designated appraiser (AACI or CRA) | MPAC, using mass-appraisal models | Real estate agent |
| **Purpose** | Market value for a specific use | Property tax base | Pricing a listing |
| **Valuation date** | Today or any past date | Legislated base date (Jan 1, 2016 for 2026 taxes) | Today |
| **Inspection** | Usually interior | Rarely | Sometimes |
| **Standards** | CUSPAP | Assessment Act | None required |
| **Independent?** | Yes — no stake in the outcome | Yes | No — tied to winning the listing |
| **Accepted by lenders, courts, CRA** | Yes | No | No |

## Why your MPAC number looks so low

Ontario postponed its province-wide reassessment, so property assessments for 2026 are still what MPAC estimated your home would have sold for on **January 1, 2016**, adjusted only for physical changes since then (like an addition). In most of the GTA, prices have moved a long way since 2016, so the assessed value is usually well below today's market value.

That doesn't mean your taxes are too low — tax rates are set relative to everyone else's assessment. What matters for fairness is whether your 2016 value is accurate compared with similar homes. If it isn't, you can challenge it (see our guide to [appealing your MPAC assessment](/blog/mpac-assessment-appeal-guide-ontario)).

## Why a CMA can be optimistic

A comparative market analysis is a useful marketing tool, but it is prepared by someone competing for your listing. It may lean on the best recent sale, skip adjustments, or quote a list price strategy rather than a market value. It has no professional standard behind it and isn't accepted for legal or tax purposes.

## When you need a real appraisal

- Refinancing, a HELOC or a private mortgage
- Settling an estate (date-of-death value)
- Separation or divorce (valuation date)
- CRA matters: change of use, non-arm's-length transfers, inherited property
- Challenging your MPAC assessment
- Pricing a private sale without an agent
- Any dispute that may reach court

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use my MPAC assessment to value an estate?
It's not a reliable measure of market value at the date of death, and it's dated to 2016. Executors normally use a certified appraisal to support the Estate Administration Tax return and the final tax return.

### Is a bank appraisal the same as an independent appraisal?
It's the same type of report, but it is ordered by and addressed to the lender. You usually can't rely on it for other purposes.

### Which number should I list my house at?
List price is a strategy decision you make with your agent. An independent appraisal tells you what the evidence supports, so you can judge whether an agent's suggested price is realistic.
