> **Quick answer:** Start with the **effective date, client and intended use** — they tell you what the report can be used for. Then check the **property description** for errors, review the **comparable sales and adjustments**, and read the **assumptions and limiting conditions**. The final value is only as good as the evidence and assumptions behind it.

## 1. Client, intended user and intended use

Who ordered the report and what it's for — mortgage lending, estate, family law, tax. A report prepared for one purpose shouldn't be relied on for another.

## 2. Effective date vs. report date

- **Effective date:** the date the value applies to (today, or a past date)
- **Report date:** when it was written

For retrospective appraisals these can be years apart.

## 3. Property description

Check carefully:

- Living area and room counts
- Basement finish and secondary suites
- Lot size and features
- Parking
- Renovations and condition

Factual errors here are the most common — and easiest to fix.

## 4. Neighbourhood and market

A summary of the area, price trends and demand. It explains why certain sales were chosen and whether market adjustments were needed.

## 5. Comparable sales and adjustments

The core of the report. Look for:

- Recent, nearby, similar sales
- Clear adjustments for differences
- Reasonable total adjustments
- An explanation of which comparables received the most weight

See [how appraisers choose comparable sales](/blog/how-appraisers-choose-comparable-sales).

## 6. Reconciliation and final value

How the appraiser weighed the evidence to arrive at one number.

## 7. Assumptions and limiting conditions

Important caveats: what wasn't inspected, assumptions about legal status, environmental conditions or condition at a past date. **Extraordinary assumptions** can change the value if they turn out to be wrong.

## 8. Certification and signature

The appraiser's designation, the statement of independence and the signature.

## Frequently asked questions

### I found an error in my report. What should I do?
Contact the appraiser (or your lender, if it's their report) with evidence. Factual corrections are routine.

### Can I use a lender's report for my estate or divorce?
Usually not. Ask for a report prepared for your purpose.

### Why does my report have so many conditions?
Professional standards require appraisers to disclose limits on their work. It's normal.
