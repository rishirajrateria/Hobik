> **Quick answer:** Adding an adult child to title can seem like an easy way to avoid probate, but it can trigger **capital gains questions**, expose the home to the **child's creditors or separation**, and cause **disputes among siblings**. If a transfer of ownership happens, its value as of the transfer date can matter for tax reporting. Get legal and tax advice first.

## Why people do it

- To reduce Estate Administration Tax (joint tenancy can pass outside the estate)
- To make the transition simpler when a parent dies
- To help a child qualify for a mortgage

## The risks

| Risk | What can happen |
|---|---|
| **Tax** | Transferring part ownership can be treated as a disposition; the child's share may not qualify for the parent's principal residence exemption |
| **Creditors** | The child's creditors could pursue their interest |
| **Family law** | The child's spouse may claim an interest in a separation |
| **Control** | You may need the child's consent to sell or refinance |
| **Sibling disputes** | Courts may need to decide whether the child holds the share for the estate or personally |

Ontario courts look at intention — whether the child was meant to benefit personally or hold the property for the estate. Clear documentation matters.

## Where valuation comes in

If ownership is actually transferred, a value at the **transfer date** may be needed for tax reporting and to support later calculations of gain. A retrospective appraisal can provide it if the transfer happened years ago.

## Alternatives to discuss with a lawyer

- Multiple wills strategies
- Trusts
- Simply paying the probate tax (often less than people expect)

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there land transfer tax when adding a child to title?
It depends on the details, including any consideration and mortgage. Ask your real estate lawyer.

### Can I undo it later?
Possibly, but the child would need to transfer their interest back, with potential tax and legal consequences.

### Do I need an appraisal?
If a taxable transfer occurs, you'll want a supportable value as of that date. Your accountant will advise.
