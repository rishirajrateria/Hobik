> **Quick answer:** Appraisers value a home by comparing it with recent sales of similar homes nearby, then adjusting for differences. The biggest factors are **location, lot, above-grade living area, layout, condition and quality, age and updates, parking, basement and suites**. Décor and furniture don't count; condition and functional features do.

## The 12 factors

1. **Location** — street, neighbourhood, school catchment, transit, busy roads, nearby commercial or industrial uses.
2. **Lot** — size, frontage, depth, shape, ravine or park backing, slopes and easements.
3. **Above-grade living area** — measured from the exterior and the biggest single size factor.
4. **Basement** — finished or unfinished, ceiling height, walk-out, separate entrance.
5. **Layout and functionality** — bedroom count, bathrooms, flow, awkward additions.
6. **Condition** — maintenance, wear and any deferred repairs.
7. **Quality of construction and finishes** — builder-grade versus custom.
8. **Effective age** — how old the home *functions* after updates, not the year it was built.
9. **Mechanical systems** — furnace, air conditioning, roof, windows, electrical, plumbing.
10. **Parking** — garage, driveway, front pad or laneway access (huge in older Toronto areas).
11. **Secondary suites and income** — legal units can add value; unpermitted ones are handled cautiously.
12. **Comparable sales** — the evidence that ties everything together.

## How comparables are used

An appraiser typically selects several recent sales as similar as possible to your home, then adjusts each one up or down for differences. For example, if a comparable has an extra bathroom, its price is adjusted down to reflect what that bathroom would add. The adjusted prices are then reconciled into a final value. Read more in [how appraisers choose comparable sales](/blog/how-appraisers-choose-comparable-sales).

## What doesn't change the value

- Furniture, staging and décor
- Paint colours (unless damaged or extremely dated)
- What you spent on renovations — the market decides what they're worth
- Your MPAC assessment
- What your neighbour *listed* for (only sold prices count)

## GTA-specific factors

- **Laneway or garden suite potential** — new as-of-right permissions in Toronto can matter for some lots
- **Teardown value** — in some neighbourhoods the land is worth more than the house
- **Condo specifics** — floor, view, exposure, parking, locker and maintenance fees
- **Older housing stock** — knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing and foundations

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a finished basement count as square footage?
Basements are usually reported separately from above-grade living area, then valued based on finish quality and market preference.

### Do pools add value in Toronto?
It depends on the neighbourhood and the buyer pool. In some areas a pool is neutral or even a negative because of maintenance and yard loss.

### Will the appraiser look at my roof and furnace?
They'll note the visible condition and age, but an appraisal is not a home inspection. Hidden defects may not be found.
