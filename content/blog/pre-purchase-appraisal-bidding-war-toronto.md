> **Quick answer:** A pre-purchase appraisal tells you what comparable sales support **before** you commit. It's most valuable when you plan to make a firm offer (no financing condition) in a competitive situation, because if the lender later appraises below your price, you must cover the gap in cash.

## The risk you're managing

When you waive a financing condition, your obligation to close doesn't depend on what the bank's appraiser thinks. If the lender values the home $80,000 below your price, that $80,000 typically comes from your savings. A pre-purchase appraisal gives you a realistic ceiling before you bid.

## When it's worth it

- You plan to offer **firm**, with no financing condition
- The home is **unique** (custom build, unusual lot, heritage, few comparables)
- Prices in the area have **moved quickly** recently
- You're stretching your **down payment** and have little cushion
- The listing price looks deliberately **low** to provoke a bidding war

## When it may not be needed

- Your offer keeps a financing condition
- The home is a common type with many recent comparable sales
- You have enough cash to absorb a gap comfortably

## Timing and access

Offer nights move fast. Book as early as possible after deciding to bid. The appraiser will need access through the listing agent, usually alongside a showing. Rush service may be available.

## How to use the number

| Your appraisal says | What it means for your bid |
|---|---|
| Value at or above your planned bid | Evidence supports your price |
| Value somewhat below | Decide how much gap you could cover in cash |
| Value well below | Consider a lower ceiling, a conditional offer, or walking away |

The appraisal doesn't tell you what to bid — it tells you what the evidence supports, so you can decide how much risk you're taking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my lender accept my pre-purchase appraisal?
Usually not; lenders order their own. Your report helps you decide, and can support a reconsideration if the lender's value seems low.

### Is a home inspection still needed?
Yes. An appraisal values the property; an inspection looks for defects. See [appraisal vs. home inspection](/blog/appraisal-vs-home-inspection).

### Can I get an appraisal if the seller won't allow extra access?
An exterior or desktop valuation may be possible, with assumptions clearly stated.
