> **Quick answer:** **Market value** is what a buyer would pay for your home *including the land*. **Replacement cost** is what it would cost to **rebuild the structure** after a total loss — land isn't included because it doesn't burn down. In Toronto, where land is a huge share of value, the two numbers can be far apart.

## Side by side

| | Market value | Replacement cost |
|---|---|---|
| Includes land | Yes | No |
| Based on | Comparable sales | Construction costs |
| Used for | Buying, selling, lending, estates, tax | Insurance coverage |
| Affected by location | Hugely | Mainly through local building costs |
| Older homes | Character may add value | Rebuilding old materials can be costly |

## Why they diverge in the GTA

A modest bungalow in a sought-after neighbourhood may have a high market value mostly because of the land. Its rebuild cost could be much lower. Conversely, a large, custom home in a lower-priced area could cost more to rebuild than it would sell for.

## Risks of mixing them up

- **Insuring for market value** — paying for coverage on land that can't be destroyed
- **Insuring for too little** — rising construction costs leave a shortfall after a major claim
- **Heritage or custom features** — specialty materials can push rebuild costs above standard estimates

## How replacement cost is usually estimated

Insurers typically use cost-estimating software based on size, construction quality, finishes and features. For unique or high-value homes, a professional replacement cost estimate can be worthwhile.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will an appraisal tell me my insurance amount?
A market value appraisal isn't an insurance estimate. Some appraisers offer cost estimates separately — ask.

### Does a renovation change my replacement cost?
Yes. Tell your insurer about significant upgrades so coverage keeps pace.

### Should I review my coverage every year?
Construction costs change. Many advisors suggest reviewing coverage regularly and after renovations.
