> **Quick answer:** Under Canadian tax rules, when you change a property's use — from your home to a rental, or from a rental to your home — you're generally treated as having **sold and re-bought it at fair market value** on that date. Elections may let you defer the gain in some situations, but you still need a reliable value as of the change date. A retrospective appraisal provides it.

## Why the date matters

The value on the change date splits the property's history into two periods:

- **Before the change:** possibly sheltered by the principal residence exemption
- **After the change:** gains may be taxable, and rental income rules apply

Get that dividing value wrong and you either pay too much tax or invite a CRA challenge.

## Common scenarios

### Home → rental
You move for work and rent out your Toronto condo. The deemed disposition happens when it becomes a rental. A special election may let you keep treating it as your principal residence for up to four years, as long as no capital cost allowance (depreciation) is claimed. You still need the value on the conversion date.

### Rental → home
You move into a property you were renting out. There's a deemed disposition at fair market value, which may trigger a gain on the rental period. An election can sometimes defer it, but there are conditions.

### Partial change
Renting out a basement suite or converting part of a home can also raise change-in-use questions — usually depending on how substantial the rental use is and whether structural changes were made.

## What your accountant will ask for

- The date the use changed
- The fair market value on that date
- Supporting evidence for the value (an appraisal)
- Whether you claimed capital cost allowance

## What a change-in-use appraisal looks like

- Effective date = the conversion date (often months or years ago)
- Comparable sales from around that date
- The property's condition at that time
- Addressed for income tax reporting purposes

## Frequently asked questions

### I converted my home to a rental five years ago and never got a value. Is it too late?
No. A retrospective appraisal can establish the value as of the conversion date using historical sales. Then talk to your accountant about filing.

### Does renting out a basement suite trigger a change in use?
Not always. It depends on the extent of the rental use and any structural changes. Get advice from an accountant.

### Can I use the MPAC assessment instead?
It reflects a 2016 valuation date for mass-appraisal purposes, not market value on your conversion date, so it's a weak substitute.
