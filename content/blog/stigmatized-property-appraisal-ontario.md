> **Quick answer:** A **stigmatized property** is one where an event — a violent death, serious crime or public notoriety — makes some buyers reluctant, even though nothing is physically wrong. The effect on value varies widely: sometimes negligible, sometimes significant, often fading over time. Appraisers estimate it using sales of similar stigmatized properties and market behaviour, and state their assumptions clearly.

## Physical vs. psychological

| Type | Examples | Can be fixed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical defect** | Mould, foundation damage, contamination | Usually, with repairs |
| **Psychological stigma** | Violent death, notorious crime, alleged haunting | No physical fix — perception changes over time |
| **Environmental stigma** | Former grow-op, nearby contamination | Partly — remediation plus time |

## What affects the size of the discount

- **Nature of the event** — natural death is usually viewed differently from violent crime
- **Publicity** — media coverage makes stigma more widely known
- **Time since the event**
- **Market strength** — in tight markets, buyers are often less deterred
- **Buyer pool** — investors may be less concerned than owner-occupiers

## How appraisers approach it

Stigma is hard to measure because comparable stigmatized sales are rare. Appraisers may:

1. Search for sales of other homes with similar histories and compare them with normal sales
2. Look at marketing time and price reductions
3. Consider buyer surveys or published research cautiously
4. State the assumptions and the range of possible impact

## Disclosure in Ontario

Disclosure obligations for psychological stigma are a legal question. Sellers should get advice from a real estate lawyer and be truthful if asked direct questions. Misrepresentation can lead to claims after closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a natural death in the home affect value?
Often very little, especially for elderly owners passing away peacefully. Circumstances and publicity matter.

### How long does stigma last?
It usually fades with time and changes of ownership, but there's no fixed rule.

### Should an estate appraisal account for stigma?
If the event is known and the market would react, the appraiser should consider it. Discuss it openly with your appraiser.
