> **Quick answer:** Canada has no inheritance tax, but when someone dies the CRA generally treats their capital property as **sold at fair market value immediately before death**. Any gain is taxed on the deceased's final return, unless it's sheltered (for example, by the principal residence exemption). That same value becomes the heir's **cost base** — so a well-supported date-of-death appraisal matters twice: once for the estate and again when the heir later sells.

## The two tax moments

1. **At death:** the deceased is deemed to dispose of the property at fair market value. A cottage or rental property often triggers a capital gain. A principal residence may be fully or partly exempt.
2. **When the heir sells:** the heir's gain is the sale price minus their cost — which generally starts at the date-of-death value.

At the current **50% inclusion rate** (the proposed increase was cancelled in 2025), half of a capital gain is added to taxable income.

## Example

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Parent bought cottage | $250,000 |
| Fair market value at death | $900,000 |
| Gain on final return | $650,000 (half taxable) |
| Heir sells three years later | $980,000 |
| Heir's gain | $80,000 (half taxable) |

If the date-of-death value were understated at $800,000, the final-return gain would shrink — but the heir's future gain would grow by the same amount, and the CRA could challenge the low value. Overstating it does the opposite. Accuracy protects everyone.

## Why a retrospective appraisal

- Value is required **as of the date of death**, not today
- It must reflect the property's condition on that date
- The CRA can ask how the value was determined
- Beneficiaries may disagree; an independent report is neutral evidence

## Common situations

- **Family cottage** passing to children — usually the largest tax exposure
- **Rental property** — capital gain plus potential recapture of depreciation
- **Principal residence** — check whether every year of ownership qualifies
- **Joint ownership with a child** — complex; get advice on beneficial ownership

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there inheritance tax in Canada?
No federal inheritance tax. Instead, there's the deemed disposition at death on the final return, and in Ontario, Estate Administration Tax on assets passing through the will.

### Can the executor just use the eventual sale price?
If the sale is soon after death in a stable market, it may be close. If months or years pass, an appraisal at the date of death is much more defensible.

### Who needs the appraisal — the estate or the heir?
Both rely on it. The estate uses it for the final return; the heir keeps it as proof of their cost base.
