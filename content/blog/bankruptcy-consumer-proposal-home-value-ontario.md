> **Quick answer:** In a consumer proposal or bankruptcy, your **home equity** — market value minus mortgages and selling costs — affects what creditors could receive. A **Licensed Insolvency Trustee** may ask for an appraisal to determine that equity. An accurate, independent value helps set a realistic proposal and protects everyone from disputes later.

## Why equity matters

Creditors generally shouldn't receive less in a proposal than they would in a bankruptcy. If you have significant equity, that equity can influence:

- How much a consumer proposal needs to offer
- Whether keeping the home is realistic in a bankruptcy
- Payments to the trustee to retain your equity

*Rules are complex — your Licensed Insolvency Trustee will explain how they apply.*

## The basic equity calculation

| | Example |
|---|---|
| Market value | $850,000 |
| Mortgage | − $640,000 |
| Estimated selling costs | − $40,000 |
| **Approximate equity** | **$170,000** |

Ontario provides certain exemptions for some assets; your trustee will advise.

## Why an independent appraisal

- Online estimates and assessments aren't reliable for this purpose
- Overstating value can make a proposal unaffordable
- Understating value can be challenged by creditors or the trustee
- A documented value supports the trustee's work and your file

## Frequently asked questions

### Will the trustee order the appraisal?
Often the trustee requests one, sometimes from an appraiser they choose. Ask what they need.

### Can a drive-by appraisal be used?
Sometimes, depending on the trustee's requirements and the property.

### Do I need an appraisal if I have no equity?
The trustee may still want evidence of value to confirm it.
