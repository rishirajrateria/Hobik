> **Quick answer:** Appraisers choose recent sales of homes as similar as possible to yours — same area, type, size, age and quality — then **adjust each sale's price** for differences (an extra bathroom, a bigger lot, better condition). The adjusted prices are weighed, with the most similar sales counting most, to reach a final value.

## What makes a good comparable

1. **Location** — same neighbourhood, ideally same street pattern and school area
2. **Recency** — sold close to the effective date
3. **Type** — detached vs. semi vs. townhouse vs. condo
4. **Size** — similar above-grade living area
5. **Age and quality** — similar construction era and finish level
6. **Condition** — similar level of updating
7. **Arm's-length sale** — a normal market transaction, not a family sale or forced sale

No comparable is perfect. The goal is the fewest and smallest adjustments.

## How adjustments work

Adjustments are made **to the comparable**, to make it look like your home:

- Comparable is *better* in some way → adjust its price **down**
- Comparable is *worse* → adjust its price **up**

### Simple example

| | Your home | Comparable A | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale price | — | $1,150,000 | |
| Bathrooms | 2 | 3 | − $25,000 |
| Garage | Yes | No | + $40,000 |
| Condition | Updated | Original | + $60,000 |
| **Adjusted price** | | | **$1,225,000** |

Adjustment amounts come from market evidence — for example, comparing similar sales with and without a garage.

## Reconciliation

After adjusting several comparables, the appraiser weighs them. A sale with small total adjustments that's very close by usually gets more weight than one that needed big corrections.

## How to sanity-check a report

- Are the comparables genuinely similar and nearby?
- Are they recent?
- Are adjustments explained, and do they seem proportional?
- Did the appraiser miss a key feature of your home?

If something looks wrong, ask the appraiser — or your lender, if it's a bank report — about a reconsideration with specific evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do active listings count as comparables?
Listings show where the market may be heading, but sold prices are the core evidence.

### Why didn't the appraiser use my neighbour's sale?
It may have been a non-arm's-length sale, very different in condition, or sold too long ago. Ask — there's usually a reason.

### How many comparables are typical?
Most residential reports use at least three sold comparables, often more.
