> **Quick answer:** A retrospective appraisal estimates a property's market value **as of a specific past date**, using sales and market conditions from that time and the property's condition on that date. It's commonly needed for estates (date of death), separations (valuation date), CRA matters (change in use, transfers), MPAC appeals (January 1, 2016) and litigation.

## When you need one

| Situation | Typical effective date |
|---|---|
| Estate administration | Date of death |
| Separation or divorce | Valuation date (usually separation) |
| CRA change in use | Date the property's use changed |
| Non-arm's-length transfer | Date of transfer |
| MPAC appeal | January 1, 2016 (for the 2026 tax year) |
| Litigation or insurance | Date of the event in dispute |

## How appraisers look backwards

1. **Market data from the period.** Sales that closed around the effective date — not today's prices.
2. **The property as it was.** If the home has been renovated, damaged or sold since, the appraiser relies on photos, listings, permits, invoices, MPAC records and owner interviews to reconstruct its condition.
3. **Market trends.** If few sales occurred close to the date, sales slightly before or after are adjusted for market movement.
4. **No hindsight.** The value reflects what was known and knowable on that date.

## What you can provide to help

- Photos from around the effective date (family photos are often useful)
- Old listing details or MLS printouts
- Renovation invoices and building permits
- Insurance records
- Any inspection reports from the time

## Is a retrospective appraisal less reliable?

Not if it's done carefully. The key is clear assumptions and good historical evidence. The report should state the effective date, the date it was written, and any assumptions about condition.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far back can an appraiser go?
There's no fixed limit, but the older the date, the harder it is to find good sales data and evidence of condition. Decades-old valuations are possible but more limited.

### Can you appraise a house that's been demolished?
Yes, with enough historical evidence — though assumptions will be stated clearly.

### Do retrospective appraisals cost more?
Usually, because of the extra research. Ask for a quote with your effective date.
