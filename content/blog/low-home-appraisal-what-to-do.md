> **Quick answer:** When a home appraises below the purchase price, your lender usually lends on the **lower** value, leaving an "appraisal gap" you must cover. Your options include adding cash, asking for a reconsideration with better comparables, trying another lender, renegotiating with the seller, or using an alternative lender. The best protection is knowing the value *before* you firm up.

## Why it happens

- **Bidding wars** push prices above recent comparable sales
- **Rising markets** where the latest sales haven't closed or been recorded yet
- **Unique homes** with few comparable sales
- **Missed features** — the appraiser didn't know about a renovation or legal suite

## How the gap affects your mortgage

Example with 20% down:

| | Expected | After low appraisal |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $1,000,000 | $1,000,000 |
| Lender's value | $1,000,000 | $950,000 |
| Max 80% loan | $800,000 | $760,000 |
| Cash needed | $200,000 | **$240,000** |

The lender caps the loan using the lower of price or appraised value, so the shortfall comes out of your pocket.

## Seven options

1. **Cover the gap with cash** — savings, a gift from family (with a gift letter) or other funds.
2. **Request a reconsideration of value** through your broker, with comparable sales and upgrade details the appraiser may have missed.
3. **Try another lender** — some will order a new appraisal. Your broker can advise.
4. **Renegotiate** — if your offer had a financing condition, you may be able to ask the seller to lower the price.
5. **Adjust your down payment structure** — with less than 20% down, insured mortgages have different rules; your broker can model it.
6. **Use an alternative or private lender** for the shortfall — usually at a higher cost.
7. **Walk away** only if your offer was conditional on financing. On a firm deal, failing to close can be very costly.

## How to prevent it

- Get a [pre-purchase appraisal](/blog/pre-purchase-appraisal-bidding-war-toronto) before offer night on homes that will bid up
- Keep a financing condition when the market allows
- Have extra cash ready if you plan to bid aggressively
- Share renovation details with the lender's appraiser through your broker

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I order my own appraisal and give it to the bank?
Usually not. Lenders use appraisers from their own panels. An independent appraisal is still useful as evidence when requesting a reconsideration and for your own decision-making.

### Does a low appraisal mean I overpaid?
Not necessarily. It means the evidence available on that date didn't support the price. In fast markets, appraisals can lag behind real buyer behaviour.

### Can the seller be forced to lower the price?
No. On a firm agreement, the seller can insist on the agreed price.
