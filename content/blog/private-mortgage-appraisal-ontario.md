> **Quick answer:** Private lenders focus on **the property's value and your equity**, more than your income. They rely on an **independent appraisal addressed to them** to set a conservative loan-to-value — commonly lower than bank limits for second mortgages. A fast, well-supported report keeps time-sensitive deals moving.

## How private lenders use value

| | Example |
|---|---|
| Appraised value | $1,000,000 |
| Lender's maximum combined LTV (example) | 75% → $750,000 |
| Existing first mortgage | − $550,000 |
| **Possible second mortgage** | **$200,000** |

Each lender sets its own limits, which vary with location, property type and risk.

## What private lenders look for

- Report **addressed to the lender** as client or intended user
- Recent effective date
- Conservative, well-supported comparables
- Clear notes on condition, legal suites and marketability
- **As-is** value, and **as-complete** if funds are for renovation or construction

## Common uses

- Debt consolidation when banks decline
- Bridge financing between purchase and sale
- Renovation or construction financing
- Short-term financing for self-employed borrowers

## Avoiding delays

1. Confirm the lender's appraisal requirements before ordering.
2. Give the appraiser the lender's exact legal name.
3. Provide access, documents and any construction plans.
4. Ask about rush timing if the deal is urgent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I reuse a bank appraisal for a private lender?
Usually not — reports are addressed to a specific client. A new report or a re-addressed one (if permitted) may be required.

### Do private lenders accept drive-by appraisals?
Some do for lower-risk files. Many prefer a full inspection.

### Is a private mortgage expensive?
Typically more costly than bank financing. Speak with a licensed mortgage broker about the full costs.
