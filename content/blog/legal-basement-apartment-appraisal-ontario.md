> **Quick answer:** A **legal, permitted** secondary suite can add real value — buyers pay for rental income and flexibility, and lenders may count the rent. An **unpermitted** suite is treated cautiously: it may be valued as finished basement space rather than an income unit, and some lenders won't recognise the rent. Paperwork is often worth more than the renovation.

## Three categories

| Status | How it's typically treated |
|---|---|
| **Legal / permitted** | Valued as an income-producing feature; rent may be considered |
| **Registered or legal non-conforming** | Generally recognised, with any limitations noted |
| **Unpermitted** | Often valued as finished basement only; risk is flagged |

## Why legality matters to value

- **Buyers' lenders** may only count rental income from legal units
- **Insurance** can be affected by unpermitted work
- **Liability and safety** — fire separation, egress windows, ceiling heights
- **Future enforcement** — a by-law complaint can force changes

The market pays for certainty. Two identical-looking basements can carry very different values.

## How appraisers value a suite

1. Confirm the status — permits, inspections, municipal records
2. Look for comparable sales of homes **with** and **without** similar suites
3. Consider rent levels for similar units
4. Note physical features: separate entrance, ceiling height, windows, kitchen, laundry, sound separation

In areas where many homes have income suites, the market difference is easier to measure.

## Paperwork that helps

- Building permit and final inspection
- Fire inspection or electrical safety certificate (where applicable)
- Current lease and rent
- Floor plan of both units

## Ontario context

Recent provincial and municipal changes have made additional residential units more widely permitted — in Toronto, up to four units are allowed as-of-right in many low-rise residential zones. Permission to build isn't the same as an existing legal unit, though; the specific suite still needs proper permits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a basement apartment always increase my appraisal?
Usually if it's legal and well built. If it's unpermitted or poorly built, the effect can be small or even negative.

### Can I legalise my suite before an appraisal?
Yes, though it takes time. Even starting the process and documenting it can help.

### Do lenders count basement rent?
Many do for legal suites, subject to their policies. Ask your broker.
