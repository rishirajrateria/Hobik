> **Quick answer:** A standard residential appraisal in Toronto usually costs a few hundred dollars plus HST. The fee goes up for large, luxury or unusual homes, multi-unit properties, valuations as of a past date (retrospective), rush delivery and anything that may go to court. A reputable appraiser will confirm the exact fee in writing before the inspection.

## What you are actually paying for

An appraisal fee is not a fee for a number. It pays for a qualified, independent professional to:

- Inspect and measure the property (usually 30–60 minutes on site)
- Research recent comparable sales and current listings in your micro-market
- Adjust those sales for differences in size, condition, lot, location and features
- Reconcile the evidence into a supported market value
- Write and sign a report that follows the Canadian Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (CUSPAP)

Most of the time is spent on research and analysis, not at your house.

## The six things that change the price

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | A lender report, an estate valuation and a court report have different content and liability. |
| **Property type** | Condos with plenty of in-building sales are usually quicker than custom homes or multiplexes. |
| **Size and complexity** | Large lots, additions, secondary suites and unique features need more analysis. |
| **Valuation date** | Retrospective appraisals (date of death, separation, change of use) require historical research. |
| **Turnaround** | Rush delivery for an offer date or closing reshuffles the appraiser's schedule. |
| **Extras** | Court testimony, additional structures and report revisions are usually quoted separately. |

## Is the cheapest quote the best value?

For a straightforward refinance, price is a fair way to compare. But when the report may be scrutinised — an estate return, a family-law equalization, a CRA filing or an MPAC appeal — the quality of the evidence matters far more than a small difference in fee. A weak report can cost you far more in taxes, a lopsided settlement or a second appraisal.

Ask every appraiser the same questions:

1. Are you a designated member of the Appraisal Institute of Canada (AACI or CRA)?
2. Will the report be addressed to the right party (you, your lender, your lawyer)?
3. What is included, and what costs extra?
4. When will I receive the signed report?

## Who pays for the appraisal?

- **Refinances and HELOCs:** usually the borrower, although some lenders absorb or reimburse the cost.
- **Estates:** the estate pays, as an administration expense.
- **Separations:** often shared between spouses, depending on your agreement.
- **Private sales and pre-listing:** the person ordering it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HST charged on appraisal fees in Ontario?
Yes. Appraisal services are taxable, so HST is added to the professional fee.

### Is an appraisal cheaper for a condo?
Often, yes. Condos usually have recent sales in the same building, which makes the comparable research more direct. Large or unusual units can still take longer.

### Does a retrospective appraisal cost more?
Usually. Valuing a home as of a past date means researching the market as it was then and confirming what the property was like on that date.

### Can I use an appraisal my lender ordered for something else?
Generally no. Reports are prepared for a named client and a specific intended use. An estate, court or CRA matter normally needs its own report.
