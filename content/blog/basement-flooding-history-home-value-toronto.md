> **Quick answer:** A past basement flood that was **properly repaired and mitigated** often has a modest effect on value, especially with documentation. Repeated flooding, visible damage, mould or difficulty getting insurance can have a larger effect. Mitigation upgrades — **backwater valves, sump pumps, downspout disconnection, grading** — help buyers and insurers feel confident.

## Why buyers care

- **Insurance** — water claims history can affect premiums and availability
- **Hidden damage** — mould, rot and damaged finishes
- **Recurrence risk** — older sewer connections and low-lying areas
- **Use of the basement** — especially if it's a finished space or secondary suite

## Mitigation that helps

| Upgrade | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Backwater valve | Prevents sewer backup into the basement |
| Sump pump (with backup) | Removes groundwater |
| Downspout disconnection | Reduces water entering foundation drains |
| Grading and window wells | Directs surface water away |
| Foundation waterproofing | Reduces seepage |

The City of Toronto has offered subsidy programs for some flood-protection devices — check current availability.

## How appraisers treat it

1. **Current condition** — is damage repaired and is the space dry?
2. **Documentation** — invoices, permits, mould remediation reports
3. **Market reaction** — sales of homes with known flood histories nearby, where available
4. **Area risk** — neighbourhoods with known basement flooding issues

## Disclosure

Sellers should get legal advice on disclosure. Known material issues that are concealed can lead to claims after closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does one past flood hurt my home's value?
If repaired and mitigated, the effect is often small. Documentation makes the biggest difference.

### Should I install a backwater valve before selling?
It can reassure buyers and insurers. Compare cost with likely benefit.

### Will the appraiser test for moisture?
Appraisers note visible signs but don't perform moisture testing. A home inspector can.
