> **Quick answer:** Energy upgrades can add value, but the market evidence is still developing. Upgrades that **replace something that needed replacing anyway** — like an old furnace or air conditioner — tend to be valued most clearly. Owned solar systems may add value; **leased** systems can complicate a sale. Documentation of costs, savings and warranties helps appraisers recognise them.

## How each upgrade is typically viewed

| Upgrade | How the market tends to treat it |
|---|---|
| **Heat pump** (replacing old HVAC) | Similar to new heating and cooling equipment; efficiency is a bonus |
| **Solar panels — owned** | May add value; evidence varies by area |
| **Solar panels — leased/financed** | Buyer must assume the contract; can be neutral or negative |
| **Attic insulation, air sealing** | Rarely visible; modest recognition |
| **New windows** | Recognised as condition and comfort improvements |
| **EV charger** | Small convenience premium, growing with EV adoption |

## Why value is hard to measure

Few comparable sales clearly differ only by energy features, and buyers vary in how much they care. Appraisers may use:

- Paired sales where available
- Energy cost savings (as an income-style cross-check)
- Cost less depreciation

## What to document

- Equipment specs and installation dates
- Invoices and any rebates received
- Utility bills before and after
- Energy audit reports (EnerGuide ratings)
- Solar production data and contracts (owned vs. leased)

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a high EnerGuide rating raise my appraisal?
It can help, especially when supported by lower bills and recent upgrades, but the market effect varies.

### Do leased solar panels affect a mortgage?
Some lenders and buyers treat leases like debt or a registered interest. Review the contract early if you plan to sell.

### Is a heat pump better for value than a new furnace?
Both refresh major systems. A heat pump may add appeal for buyers focused on efficiency and cooling.
