> **Quick answer:** Make every room accessible, prepare a dated list of renovations and their costs, and have your survey, floor plan, tax bill and any permits ready. Preparation won't inflate the value — appraisers follow the market — but it makes sure nothing that adds value is missed and the report isn't delayed.

## The 10-step checklist

1. **Clear the path.** Unlock every room, the garage, the furnace room and the attic hatch. Locked doors mean assumptions.
2. **List your upgrades.** Kitchen, bathrooms, roof, windows, furnace, air conditioning, basement finishing — with the year and approximate cost.
3. **Gather documents.** Survey, floor plan, recent MPAC notice, building permits and any final inspections.
4. **Note income.** If you have a legal secondary suite, share the lease and rent. Unpermitted units are treated differently, so be upfront.
5. **Fix the obvious.** Burnt-out bulbs, dripping taps, missing trim and broken handles are cheap to fix and avoid "deferred maintenance" notes.
6. **Make mechanicals visible.** Clear space around the furnace, water heater and electrical panel.
7. **Tidy, don't stage.** Clutter doesn't change value, but it can hide floors, walls and features in photos.
8. **Secure pets.** It keeps the inspection quick and safe.
9. **Share local knowledge.** If a neighbour sold recently and you know details (renovated or not), mention it. Appraisers verify, but leads help.
10. **Be available, not hovering.** Answer questions at the start, then let the appraiser measure and photograph.

## What appraisers actually look at

- **Location:** street, proximity to transit, schools, busy roads and amenities
- **Lot:** size, shape, frontage, ravine or backing onto commercial uses
- **Size and layout:** above-grade living area is measured separately from the basement
- **Condition and quality:** finishes, workmanship, wear and maintenance
- **Age and updates:** effective age matters more than the year built
- **Comparable sales:** what similar homes nearby actually sold for

## What doesn't move the needle

- Furniture, art and décor
- Personal taste (paint colours, unless very dated or damaged)
- How much you paid for your renovation — value is what the market will pay for it, not its cost

## Documents to have ready

| Document | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Survey or plan | Confirms lot size, easements and structures |
| Renovation list | Supports condition and effective age |
| Building permits | Confirms additions and suites are legal |
| MPAC notice | Cross-checks property details on record |
| Condo status certificate (condos) | Fees, reserve fund, parking and locker |

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I be home during the appraisal?
It helps to be there for the first few minutes to provide access and answer questions. After that, the appraiser will work through the home independently.

### Will a deep clean increase my appraisal?
Not directly. Condition matters; cleanliness doesn't. But a tidy home makes it easier to see and photograph the features that do count.

### Do unfinished renovations hurt the value?
They can. An incomplete kitchen or bathroom is usually valued "as is," with a deduction for the cost to complete. If timing allows, finish projects before the inspection.

### Can I give the appraiser comparable sales?
Yes. Appraisers independently verify every sale, but they appreciate local insight, especially about renovations not visible from listing photos.
