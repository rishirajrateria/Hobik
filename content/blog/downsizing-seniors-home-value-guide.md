> **Quick answer:** If you're thinking of downsizing, an **independent appraisal** early in the process tells you what your home is realistically worth — before anyone is trying to sell you a service. It helps you budget for your next home or care, compare offers from family or agents, and make decisions without pressure.

## Why value first

- **Budgeting** — your home equity may fund your next home, care or retirement income
- **Comparing options** — condo, retirement residence, living with family, staying and renovating
- **Family conversations** — a neutral number reduces disagreements
- **Protection** — helps you recognise unrealistic offers or listing prices

## Questions to answer early

1. What is my home worth today, as-is?
2. What would small repairs or updates add (if anything)?
3. What will my next home or residence cost each month?
4. How will moving affect taxes, benefits and estate plans?

## Selling to family

If a child or relative will buy the home, an independent appraisal protects everyone, especially other heirs. Selling well below market can create disputes — or tax issues — later.

## Before listing

- Fix safety issues and obvious repairs
- Avoid major renovations unless clearly worthwhile
- Gather documents: survey, permits, upgrade receipts
- Consider a pre-listing inspection

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I renovate before selling?
Usually only small repairs and refreshes. Ask an appraiser or agent before spending heavily.

### Does selling my principal residence trigger tax?
Generally a principal residence is sheltered by the exemption, but confirm with an accountant — especially if you've owned more than one property.

### Can a power of attorney sell my home if needed?
Yes, if you've appointed an attorney for property. See [selling a parent's home under power of attorney](/blog/power-of-attorney-selling-parents-home-appraisal).
