> **Quick answer:** A completed, permitted garden or laneway suite usually adds value through **rental income, multi-generational flexibility and extra living space** — but the added value is rarely equal to what it cost to build. The *potential* to build one is a minor factor unless the lot is unusually well suited and the market clearly pays for it.

## The rules in brief

Toronto permits **laneway suites** (on lots next to a public lane) and **garden suites** (in backyards, without lane access) in most residential zones, subject to size, setback and separation rules. These sit alongside the city's as-of-right permissions for up to four units in the main building. Always check current zoning for a specific lot.

## Cost vs. value

Building a suite is expensive, and appraisers value what the **market** pays, not what you spent:

| Factor | Effect on value |
|---|---|
| Rental income potential | Often the biggest driver |
| Quality and size | Larger, better-built suites attract more |
| Separate servicing | Independent utilities and access help |
| Neighbourhood demand | Rental and family demand vary by area |
| Comparable sales | Limited data in some areas — appraisers may use income and cost approaches as support |

## How appraisers approach it

1. **Sales comparison** — homes with completed suites, where available
2. **Income approach** — what the suite rents for, and how investors value that income
3. **Cost approach** — build cost less depreciation, as a cross-check

Because suites are still relatively new in many neighbourhoods, good appraisals explain which approach carried the most weight.

## Potential isn't value (mostly)

A large lot with lane access *could* get a suite, but buyers discount for cost, time, approvals and risk. Appraisers typically treat potential as a modest feature unless there's clear market evidence buyers pay for it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do lenders count rent from a garden suite?
Many do once it's complete and legally rented, depending on the lender's policies.

### Is a laneway suite taxed separately?
It's assessed by MPAC as part of the property, which can increase your assessment.

### Should I build a suite before selling?
Only if the numbers work — compare build cost against the likely increase in value and the time involved.
