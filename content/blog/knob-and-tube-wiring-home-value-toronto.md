> **Quick answer:** Active knob-and-tube wiring rarely makes a Toronto home unsellable, but it can **narrow the buyer pool**, complicate **insurance and financing**, and lead buyers to discount for replacement cost. Appraisers usually reflect it as a condition issue — often comparable to the cost and disruption of rewiring — guided by how the local market treats similar homes.

## Why it shows up in Toronto

Many homes in older neighbourhoods — the Annex, Leslieville, Riverdale, the Junction, Cabbagetown — were built before modern wiring standards. Some still have active knob-and-tube circuits, often alongside newer wiring added over the decades.

## Why it matters

- **Insurance:** some insurers decline, surcharge or require an electrical inspection and replacement plan
- **Financing:** lenders may want proof the home is insurable
- **Safety perception:** no grounding, brittle insulation and capacity limits worry buyers
- **Renovation cost:** rewiring plaster-and-lath homes involves opening walls

## Other legacy systems appraisers watch for

| System | Common concern |
|---|---|
| Galvanized steel plumbing | Corrosion, reduced pressure |
| Lead water service pipe | Health concerns; replacement programs exist |
| Older oil tanks (active or abandoned) | Environmental and insurance issues |
| Original single-pane windows | Efficiency and comfort |
| Aluminum branch wiring (some 1960s–70s homes) | Connection safety concerns |

## How appraisers treat it

1. Note the condition (often from the owner, an inspection report or an electrical certificate)
2. Compare with similar homes — were comparables updated or original?
3. Adjust for the difference, using market evidence and replacement cost as a guide

If wiring has been fully replaced, have the Electrical Safety Authority (ESA) documentation ready — it helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to replace knob-and-tube before selling?
Not legally in most cases, but insurability affects buyers. Many sellers either replace it or price accordingly.

### How do I know if my home has it?
An electrician or home inspector can check visible wiring in the basement and attic. Partial knob-and-tube is common.

### Does replacing it increase my appraisal?
It removes a negative condition factor, which can support a higher value — though the gain may not equal the cost.
